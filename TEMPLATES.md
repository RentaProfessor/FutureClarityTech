# Ready-to-send asks

The highest-leverage messages in the whole playbook are the ones to people
who already like you. 69% of clients say they'd gladly refer their provider;
~75% of the time nobody asks (Hinge Research). Send these this week.

## To The White Squirrel (and adapt for Captain Fripp's)

**The review + testimonial ask** (send after any normal check-in):

> Hi [name] — quick favor that would mean a lot. I'm growing FutureClarity
> and two things help more than anything:
>
> 1. A Google review — takes two minutes: [Google review link once GBP exists]
> 2. Two sentences I can quote on our site about what working together was
>    like (I'll send you exactly what I put up before it goes live).
>
> And zero pressure either way — happy to keep looking after the site
> regardless.

**The referral ask** (separate message, a week or two later):

> One more thing — most of our work comes through word of mouth. If you know
> one shop owner who's been putting off fixing their website, I'd love an
> intro. Same deal they'd get from us as you did: a free working demo of
> their actual site before any money changes hands.

## Phone script skeleton (for call touches in the sequencer pipeline)

Target: 82 seconds — the average successful cold call is short (Cognism).

1. "Hi, is this [name]? — I'll be quick, I know you're mid-service/mid-shift."
2. One sentence, the observation: "I looked up [business] and your
   menu's a PDF that doesn't really work on phones."
3. One sentence, the offer: "I run a small web studio here in LA — we build
   a free working demo of your actual site before you pay anything."
4. Interest CTA, not a meeting ask: "Worth me sending the link?" (email
   captured → the sequencer takes over) — or if in person is easier:
   "I'm nearby — can I swing by with it on my phone?"
5. If no: "No problem — want me to just email it so you have it?" Log the
   call either way (`POST /prospects/:id/called`).

## Walk-in one-liner (top-5 targets each batch)

> "Hi — I build websites for [restaurants/shops] here in LA. I made a working
> one for a place like yours; it's on my phone, takes 30 seconds to see.
> Who handles the website here?"

Then show the demo, leave the observation + demo URL on a card or a text,
and enroll them in the sequencer when you get an email address.
